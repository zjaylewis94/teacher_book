Sumi-e

(less is more)

Brush + ink
only!

Sumi-e is Japanese ink painting. Masters like Sesshu trained for years to paint a whole stalk of bamboo in a handful of strokes. Your job: paint one subject with as few strokes as possible, so every stroke has to count.

01What it looks like
Load the brushDip in light ink, then just the tip in dark. Now one stroke goes dark to light.
One stroke, one tryNever go back over a stroke. Leave a shaky one alone.
Empty space countsWhite paper is part of the painting. In Japanese it's ma.
Paint the spiritSkip the details. Show how it bends and grows.
02The rules
  • Brush and black ink only. No pencil, pen or Sharpie on the final.
  • 25 strokes max. Count them as you paint.
  • Leave at least 1/3 of the paper empty.
  • Subject: bamboo, orchid, plum blossom, chrysanthemum, or your own idea.
  • Sign it and add a red seal.
Rehearse it. Sumi-e is a performance. Paint your whole design 3 times on scrap paper before you touch the final. The 4th time is the one that counts.

04How it's graded 8 pts · 2 = yes · 1 = partly · 0 = missing
2 ptsPlanning: prelim done, 3 thumbnails, best one circled.
2 ptsFew strokes: 25 or fewer, counted, none gone over twice.
2 ptsBrush control: 3+ ink tones and dark-to-light loaded strokes.
2 ptsComposition: 1/3 empty, subject reads clearly, signed + sealed.

Pen + Ink

(intro worksheet)

Ink is the bold, brave cousin of the pencil. There's no eraser, so every mark you make stays on the page. That sounds scary, but it's why ink looks so confident: comics, tattoos, posters and street art all live here. This worksheet is about meeting the tools and finding out how ink behaves.

01The ink family
02Permanent vs. water-based

Permanent

Sharpie, Micron, India ink. Once it's dry, water can't move it. The bottom half got water: no change.

Water-based

Washable markers, Flair pens, most brush pens. Water melts the line into a soft wash (bottom half).

Ink isn't only black. Water it down for grays (a wash), grab colored inks and markers, or let a water-based line bleed into a soft shadow on purpose.
03Handling ink
Pull, don't pushpushing makes the tip skip and snag
Light touchpressing hard bends a fineliner tip for good
Let it drywet ink smears. Rest your hand on scrap paper
Turn the paperso every line is a comfortable pull
Golden rule: Sketch light in pencil, then ink what you mean. One confident line beats five shaky ones. Made a mistake? Ink can't be erased, so make it part of the drawing.
04Ink vocab
Line weight: how thick or thin a line is.
Nib: the metal tip of a dip pen.
Tip size: a fineliner's number. Bigger number, thicker line.
Bleed: ink spreading past your line or through the paper.
Chisel tip: a flat, angled marker tip. Thin on the edge, fat on the side.
Wash: ink thinned with water to make grays.
Permanent: water can't move it once it's dry.
Ghosting: practicing a stroke in the air before you touch the paper.
Tier 1 · Skill builder · 2 points

Ink warm-ups

Complete all five boxes. Pen only: no pencil first, no erasing. This isn't about making something pretty. It's about finding out what each tool does.

1Tip test strip

Pick five different pens or markers. In each box, write the pen's name, draw one straight line, then fill the small square solid. Which ones fill a solid black, and which leave streaks?

2Thin side, fat side

One marker, three lines. Write your name with the point, then with the flat side of the chisel. In the last box, twist the chisel slowly as you pull, so the line turns from thin to fat (a ribbon).

5Smear + bleed test

A: draw a thick line and swipe your finger across it right away. B: draw another, count to 30, then swipe. C: hold a Sharpie still on the paper for 3 seconds. Then flip your paper over.
